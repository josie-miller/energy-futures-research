# The physics behind the emerging energy technologies: what has been measured versus what deployment needs

*Stage 2 evidence base, as of 24 October 2026. Companion table: tech_evidence.csv (61 rows, 8 technology clusters). Status tags: **measured** (experiment or operating data), **modelled** (simulation or projection), **company claim** (not independently verified).*

Across eight technology clusters, one pattern holds. Every emerging technology has passed its physics proof of principle. None has yet passed the engineering metric that decides whether it can carry load. The single exception is orbital compute, which meets its thresholds on chip radiation tolerance but still has to close on mass and cost. Working down the list, the remaining gap ranges from about 1.6× (tidal stream cost) to about 100× (Mars oxygen production at human-mission scale). For the mix model, this means firm-power options such as fusion, thorium breeding and long-duration storage should be treated as upside options with explicit break-even triggers. They should not be counted as baseline supply. The technologies that can be counted on now are silicon PV, wind, Li-ion batteries, existing fission and conventional geothermal, and their limits are systems limits (reliability across weather years, construction cost), not physics limits.

## Earth: generation and firming

### Fusion: ignition proven, power plant unproven

The governing figure of merit for fusion is the Lawson criterion, often written as the fuel triple product nTτ. For deuterium–tritium (D‑T) fuel, the minimum triple product for ignition is about 2.9×10²¹ m⁻³·keV·s at 14 keV. The best tokamak shots compiled in the peer-reviewed record reach about 6×10²⁰, roughly 4.8× short ([Wurzel & Hsu 2022](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0083990)). Magnetic confinement has produced large absolute yields: JET's final D‑T campaign released 69 MJ in a 5.2 s pulse from 0.2 mg of fuel, an average of about 13 MW of fusion power. The plasma still needed more heating energy than it produced, so the scientific gain stayed below one (UKAEA/EUROfusion 2024 release). Long-pulse confinement is advancing separately. W7‑X, a stellarator, now holds the triple-product record for discharges longer than 30 seconds (43 s in May 2025; IPP release), building on its earlier demonstration of reduced neoclassical transport ([Beidler et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03687-w)). KSTAR has sustained 100-million-kelvin ion temperatures in a fast-ion-assisted regime ([Han et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05008-1)).

Inertial fusion crossed scientific breakeven first. NIF reported target gain 0.72 and Lawson-criterion ignition in 2021 ([Abu-Shawareb et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevlett.129.075001)). It then reported target gain 1.5 (3.1 MJ from 2.05 MJ of laser light) in December 2022 ([Abu-Shawareb et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevlett.132.065102)), and LLNL reports a record 8.6 MJ from 2.08 MJ in April 2025, a target gain of 4.13. Target gain is not wall-plug gain. NIF's yield is about 2.6% of the energy stored in its capacitor banks. That figure is our calculation using the roughly 330 MJ driver energy listed in a 2025 preprint; we did not independently verify the capacitor energy for the record shot. Proposals for inertial fusion energy call for target gains of 30 to 100 (arXiv 2405.09912, preprint). The physics of capsule compression and ignition is reviewed in [Betti & Hurricane 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphys3736) and [Hurricane et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1103/revmodphys.95.025005).

The next magnetic milestone is SPARC. It is a 12.2 T compact tokamak designed for Q>2 under conservative assumptions and projected to reach Q≈11 and 140 MW of fusion power at nominal confinement ([Creely et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0022377820001257)). Independent transport modelling finds Q≈9 in standard H-mode ([Rodriguez-Fernandez et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0022377820001075)). These are projections. Secondary trackers report SPARC as about 80% built, with first plasma targeted for 2027 after slips from 2025 and 2026. The ARC plant concept that would follow is described in [Sorbom et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fusengdes.2015.07.008).

The binding gaps after plasma gain are engineering problems, and none has been closed experimentally:
- **Tritium fuel cycle.** A 1 GW fusion plant burns about 56 kg of tritium per year (our calculation from 17.6 MeV per reaction). Self-sufficiency requires a burn-fraction × fuelling-efficiency product above 2%, tritium processing times of 1–4 hours and plant availability above 50% to succeed with reasonable confidence. The start-up inventory for a 3 GW plant is about 11 kg ([Abdou et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-4326/abbf35)). Civilian tritium supply is limited, and full-power D‑T operation on ITER has slipped to 2035 or later ([Pearson et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fusengdes.2018.04.090)).
- **Neutron damage to materials.** Fusion structures must survive damage approaching 200 displacements per atom along with transmuted helium and hydrogen ([Zinkle & Snead 2014](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-matsci-070813-113627); [Knaster et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphys3735)). No fusion-spectrum neutron source yet exists to qualify materials to that level; IFMIF‑DONES is the European answer ([Ibarra et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-4326/ab0d57)).

Fusion cost analyses remain contingent on these unknowns ([Entler et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.energy.2018.03.130); [Lindley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2023.113511)). Field-reversed configurations are far behind on the same metric: TAE's C‑2W entries in the Wurzel & Hsu compilation sit near 10¹⁶–10¹⁷, about four orders of magnitude below the best tokamaks. Helion's 50 MWe target for 2028 is a company claim, and Helion has published no peer-reviewed triple product or net-electricity result.

**Verdict for the mix model: option, not baseline.** Credit fusion with zero firm capacity before about 2035. Add it as a sensitivity case triggered by three demonstrations: SPARC Q>1, an integrated breeding blanket with a measured tritium breeding ratio above 1, and a 14 MeV materials qualification campaign. Plausible earliest grid power is from ARC-class plants in the early-to-mid 2030s if all three succeed.

### Fission: the physics is settled; construction is the binding constraint

Fission's limits are no longer physical. The empirical record shows that construction cost, not fuel or neutronics, sets its competitiveness. US data spanning five decades show that later plants were *more* expensive than first-of-a-kind ones. Soft factors such as labour supervision drove more than half of the 1976–1987 cost rise, and labour productivity at recent plants was up to 13× below industry expectations ([Eash-Gates et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joule.2020.10.001)). France showed similar negative learning during its scale-up ([Grübler 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2010.05.003)). A wider dataset of 349 reactors in seven countries finds cost trajectories differing by country, with South Korea a counterexample of declining costs ([Lovering et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2016.01.011)). That interpretation is disputed ([Koomey et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2016.03.052)). Lead time and standardisation are the measurable levers ([Berthélemy & Rangel 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2015.03.015); [Portugal‑Pereira et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2018.05.041)). Vogtle 3&4, the latest US build, came in at roughly $35 billion over about 130 months. An MIT assessment projects $8,300–10,375/kW for a next pair of AP1000s built in 80–96 months (MIT CANES 2024).

Small modular reactors (SMRs) trade economy of scale for hoped-for economy of series. Systematic reviews find that trade unproven ([Mignacca & Locatelli 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rser.2019.109519); [Steigerwald et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.energy.2023.128204)). One peer-reviewed analysis finds that water-, molten-salt- and sodium-cooled SMR designs would increase the volume of waste needing disposal by factors of 2 to 30 ([Krall et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2111833119)); the result is contested by vendors.

The strongest Gen-IV evidence comes from China:
- **HTR-PM.** This 210 MWe plant uses two 250 MWth pebble-bed modules with a 750 °C outlet ([Zhang et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eng.2016.01.020)). It is operating and has published loss-of-cooling tests demonstrating passive heat removal ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joule.2024.06.014)). TRISO fuel performance underpinning this class is documented in the US AGR programme ([Demkowicz et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nucengdes.2017.09.005)).
- **TMSR-LF1.** This 2 MWt thorium molten-salt reactor reported conversion of thorium to fissile uranium-233 in November 2025, at a conversion ratio of about 0.1 against the roughly 1 that a breeder needs (World Nuclear News/SINAP). No peer-reviewed operating data have been released. Molten-salt reactor background is in [Serp et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pnucene.2014.02.014).

Existing reactors can also follow load, which raises their system value alongside renewables ([Jenkins et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2018.03.002)).

**Verdict: baseline for existing reactors and repeat builds in high-execution regions; option for SMRs and thorium.** For model inputs, use a regional capital-cost distribution, not a point value. For Western first-of-a-kind builds, use roughly $8–11k/kW and 7–11 years. China and Korea need region-specific values; that research is still open.

### Solar PV: deployment is proven; tandem cells are limited by durability

Single-junction efficiency is bounded by detailed balance, about 30% in the original Shockley–Queisser blackbody treatment ([Shockley & Queisser 1961](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1736034)). For a real silicon cell the limit is 29.43% ([Richter et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1109/jphotov.2013.2270351)). Perovskite–silicon tandems break that ceiling. LONGi's 35.5% two-terminal cell was certified by ESTI in July 2026 (company announcement with independent certification), and the record tables are maintained by [Green et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/pip.3919). The binding gap is durability. The best published outdoor record for a monolithic tandem is 80% of initial efficiency retained after one year ([Babics et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrp.2023.101280)). Bankable silicon modules lose about 0.75% per year, about 0.48% in cool climates and about 0.88% in hot ones, across 7.2 GW of US fleet data ([Jordan et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/pip.3566); [Jordan et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/pip.2744)). Stability reporting now follows the ISOS protocols ([Khenkin et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-019-0529-5)).

Mature silicon PV is not limited by physics. Energy use per watt fell 12–13% per doubling of capacity, and the industry passed cumulative energy break-even between 1997 and 2018 ([Louwen et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms13728)). About 25% annual growth looks feasible for another decade ([Haegel et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adf6957)). The constraints are materials such as silver and indium ([Zhang et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1039/d1ee01814k)) and how costs fall with deployment ([Kavlak et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2018.08.015)). Forecasting methods back-tested on more than 50 technologies favour continued rapid solar, wind and battery deployment over a nuclear-led path on cost ([Way et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joule.2022.08.009)).

**Verdict: silicon is baseline; tandems are an option** that becomes baseline once multi-year field data show silicon-like degradation rates.

### Wind and storage: the binding gap is reliability across weather years

Wind has hard physical ceilings. Extractable global land wind power is estimated at 17–38 TW ([Miller et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.5194/esd-2-1-2011)). Realised US power densities are 0.5 W/m² for wind and 5.4 W/m² for solar ([Miller & Keith 2018](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/aae102)). Wake losses grow as turbines cluster ([Lundquist et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-018-0281-2)). European fleet capacity factors average 24.2%, rising to 32.4% in Britain ([Staffell & Pfenninger 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.energy.2016.08.068)). Experts expect further cost declines ([Wiser et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-021-00810-z)).

Storage is improving quickly. Li-ion cell prices have fallen about 97% since 1991, about 13% per year, with a learning rate of 20–24% per doubling ([Ziegler & Trancik 2021](https://doi.org/10.1039/d0ee02681f)). Degradation depends strongly on chemistry and cycling conditions ([Preger et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1149/1945-7111/abae37)). The problem is duration, not batteries:
- **Hours met.** Across 42 countries and 39 weather years, wind–solar systems with no storage meet demand in 72–91% of hours, and 83–94% with 12 hours of storage. Hundreds of unmet hours remain even in the best cases ([Tong et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-26355-z); [Shaner et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1039/c7ee03029k)).
- **Seasonal gaps.** In a 100%-renewable Germany, the period that sets storage needs stretches to 12 weeks, requiring 36 TWh of storage ([Ruhnau & Qvist 2022](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ac4dc8)).
- **Cost thresholds.** Long-duration storage must cost ≤$20/kWh of energy capacity to cut system costs by at least 10%, and ≤$1/kWh to displace firm generation entirely ([Sepulveda et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-021-00796-8)). Similar storage cost thresholds appear in [Ziegler et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joule.2019.10.014) and [Schmidt et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joule.2018.12.008). Adiabatic compressed-air projects report about $120/kWh (preprint), roughly 6× above the threshold.
- **Value of firm power.** Firm low-carbon generation lowers the cost of fully decarbonised grids by 10–62% ([Sepulveda et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joule.2018.08.006)). The evidence on integration costs is reviewed in [Heptonstall & Gross 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-020-00695-4), and pumped-hydro sites are mapped globally in [Stocks et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joule.2020.11.015).

**Verdict: baseline, but only with a firm-power complement.** This is the strongest empirical case for keeping fission, geothermal and hydro in the mix.

### Geothermal: one strong field result, little independent review

The thermodynamic ceiling is low. At the 169 °C produced at Project Red, the Carnot limit is about 35%. Real binary plants recover a fraction of that ([Zarrouk & Moon 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geothermics.2013.11.001)). Fervo's 2023 Nevada pilot is the key measured result for horizontal enhanced geothermal systems (EGS). A 37-day crossflow test reached 63 L/s, 336 °F (169 °C) and 3.5 MWe ([Norbeck & Latimer 2023](https://doi.org/10.31223/x52x0b), preprint). That implies roughly 12% heat-to-power conversion, our calculation assuming 60 °C reinjection.

Fervo's company figures for the Utah site show rapid progress:
- a Cape Station well test above 10 MWe per well at 107 kg/s;
- the first 33 MW block commissioning in Q4 2026;
- a 21-day spud-to-total-depth well in a 460 °F resource.

No independent reservoir review exists, and thermal drawdown remains the open question. Earlier EGS experience at Soultz ([Genter et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crte.2010.01.006)) and the geology characterised at Utah FORGE ([Jones et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geothermics.2024.103054)) frame the uncertainty.

Induced seismicity is the social-licence risk. The Mw 5.5 Pohang earthquake was definitively triggered by EGS stimulation ([Ellsworth et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1785/0220190102); [Grigoli et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat2010); [Kim et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat6081)). Basel was abandoned after felt events ([Häring et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geothermics.2008.06.002)). Traffic-light protocols are the standard mitigation ([Bommer et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enggeo.2005.11.002)).

Superhot rock is real but not yet producing. IDDP‑2 reached 426 °C and 34 MPa at 4.5 km ([Friðleifsson et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.5194/sd-23-1-2017)). Closed-loop designs avoid stimulation; their techno-economic limits are analysed in ([Beckers et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geothermics.2021.102318)). Flexible operation, using the reservoir itself as storage, raises EGS system value ([Ricks et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2022.118807); [Ricks et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-023-01437-y)). The global technical potential is large ([Aghahosseini & Breyer 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2020.115769)).

**Verdict: emerging baseline in the US West.** Use about 10 MWe per well and 85–90% capacity factor as company-reported upper cases, and add a decline-rate sensitivity.

### Ocean: tidal is closest to market; wave is a large resource with few measurements

The ocean resource is large but modest relative to demand:
- **Wave.** The global gross wave resource is 2.11 ± 0.05 TW, about 18,500 TWh/yr, of which 4.6% is extractable with a Pelamis-type array ([Gunn & Stock-Williams 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.renene.2012.01.101)). That is about 850 TWh/yr, roughly 3% of 2025 global electricity use (our calculation). Variability is mapped in [Reguero et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2015.03.114).
- **OTEC (ocean thermal energy conversion).** Global net power peaks at about 14 TW in a high-resolution ocean model, and 7 TW is achievable without large ocean-temperature effects. An earlier coarse-model estimate was about 30 TW ([Rajagopalan & Nihous 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.renene.2012.07.014)). The Carnot limit for a 20–24 K temperature difference is only 7–8%, and the usable difference is 10–25 °C ([Vega 2002](https://doi.org/10.4031/002533202787908626)).
- **Salinity gradients.** Pressure-retarded osmosis can extract 0.75 kWh per m³ of mixed seawater and river water ([Yip & Elimelech 2012](https://doi.org/10.1021/es300060m); [Logan & Elimelech 2012](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11477)).

Tidal channels have a Betz-like extraction limit of 20–24% of peak head times peak flux ([Garrett & Cummins 2005](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.2005.1494)), and array blockage reduces this further ([Vennell 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.renene.2012.04.018); [Neill et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0069452)).

Measured output is small but real. Tidal stream farms have produced 127 GWh cumulatively to 2025 (Ocean Energy Europe 2026). The UK practical tidal resource is about 34 TWh/yr. Learning at 17% could cut today's roughly £240/MWh to below £150/MWh after 124 MW of deployment ([Coles et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.2021.0469)). Tidal output is predictable and complements wind and solar ([Pennock et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2022.119276)).

Wave energy has far fewer measurements. Babarit's capture-width database draws almost entirely on tank tests and numerical models rather than sea trials ([Babarit 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.renene.2015.02.049)), and survivability and reliability are the main design problems ([Coe et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3390/en11010004)). Model-scale extreme-wave testing of CorPower's C4 device is published ([Venugopal & Tan 2024](https://doi.org/10.1115/omae2024-127861)). Panthalassa's wave-powered compute nodes have no independent data, and the 2026 pilot is a company target. The ocean-cooled data-centre concept itself has peer-reviewed precedent in Microsoft's Natick trials ([Cutler et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1109/mspec.2017.7864753)).

**Verdict: niche for the grid in high-resource regions (UK, France, Canada, Korea); open option for offshore compute.** Test tidal in Stage 3 at £150–240/MWh with about 40% capacity factor. Test wave only as an offshore-compute case, using a break-even on maintenance cost.

## Beyond Earth

### Earth orbit: solar suits power used in orbit; beaming power down is far from viable

In a dawn–dusk sun-synchronous orbit, a square metre of array collects 5.4–6.7× more energy per year than the same area on the ground at 20–25% capacity factor (our calculation). The physics behind solar-powered orbital compute is sound. Rejecting the heat is the other half. By Stefan–Boltzmann, a two-sided radiator at emissivity 0.9 sheds about 1,210 W/m² at 330 K. SpaceX's stated 1,400 W/m² radiators therefore need a radiator temperature of about 342 K (our calculation), which is plausible but hot for chip cooling. Commercial AI accelerators survived a 5-year total ionising dose with no permanent failures, and a learning-curve analysis suggests launch costs of $200/kg or less by the mid-2030s ([Agüera y Arcas et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joule.2026.102678)). SpaceX's 100 kW per tonne figure implies 100 W/kg for the whole satellite; it is a company claim. Perovskite cells tolerate proton doses about 1,000× higher than crystalline silicon ([Lang et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.201603326); [Verduci et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/aenm.202200125)). The standard reference on III-V cell radiation tolerance is [Yamaguchi 2001](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0927-0248%2800%2900344-5).

Beaming power to Earth has a long design history ([Glaser 1968](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.162.3856.857); [Mankins et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2012-3978); [Jaffe & McSpadden 2013](https://doi.org/10.1109/jproc.2013.2252591)), but far less demonstration:
- Caltech's MAPLE achieved the first documented wireless power transfer in space and beamed a detectable signal to the ground. That was detection, not net power delivered ([Ayling et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2024.08.006)).
- Ground rectennas reach 70–80% conversion efficiency, and 100 kW-class millimetre-wave transmitters exist ([Rodenbeck et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1109/jmw.2020.3033992); [Shinohara 2011](https://doi.org/10.1109/mmm.2011.942732)).
- NASA's 2024 study models space-based solar power at $0.61–1.59/kWh, 12–30× above utility solar.
- Earlier re-evaluations of space solar power for Earth are summarised in [Landis 2006](https://doi.org/10.1109/wcpec.2006.279877).

**Verdict: in-orbit use of solar is viable on physics, with mass and cost as the open variables; beaming to Earth is not a 2050 grid resource.**

### Moon and Mars: the darkness and dust cases favour fission

Lunar polar illumination is highly site-specific ([Mazarico et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2010.10.030); [Gläser et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2014.08.013)). At non-polar sites the 14.75-day night drops surface temperatures to about 95 K before sunrise ([Williams et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2016.08.012)). On Mars, the 2018 global dust storm drove optical depth to 8.5 and cut surface ultraviolet by 97% for weeks ([Guzewich et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018GL080839)). Lofted dust particles were about 3× typical size ([Lemmon et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019gl084407)). Dust accumulation on arrays is a long-documented problem ([Landis 1996](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0094-5765%2896%2900088-4); [Stella et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1109/pvsc.2005.1488209); [Lorenz et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2021.114468)).

Space fission has passed one integrated nuclear test. KRUSTY ran for 28 hours at 1.5–5 kWth, and its Stirling converters ran at about 35% component efficiency and about 25% system efficiency ([Poston et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1080/00295450.2020.1730673); [Gibson et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2018-4973)). The 40 kWe lunar surface concept is about 30× larger in power and needs a roughly 10-year life ([Oleson et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.13182/nets22-38629)). NASA trade studies found solar lighter for a small Mars pathfinder (1,116–2,396 kg against 2,686 kg). Its earlier crewed-mission study favoured fission for reliability ([Rucker et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2016-5452)). MOXIE produced oxygen on Mars at about 1% of the scale a human mission needs ([Hoffman et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2015-4561); [Hoffman et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abp8636)).

**Verdict: fission plus solar is the baseline for continuous Moon and Mars loads. Polar lunar sites with near-continuous light are the exception where solar plus storage can win.**

## What the evidence means for the mix model

The evidence supports the handoff file's working answer: cheap variable generation plus dependable supply for the hours it cannot cover. It also sharpens that answer in three places.
1. Evidence on reliability across weather years, not on generation cost, is what justifies keeping fission, geothermal and hydro.
2. Fusion, thorium breeding, tandem PV and long-duration storage each have a single measurable trigger that should switch them from option to baseline in the model: Q_eng>1 with a measured tritium breeding ratio above 1; a conversion ratio of about 1; multi-year degradation of 0.8%/yr or less; and $20/kWh or less of energy capacity.
3. The ocean case is strongest where it delivers something other than grid electricity. That makes Stage 4's land-versus-sea-versus-orbit compute comparison the right test for the ocean hypothesis.

Open gaps in this evidence base:
- Region-specific fission costs for China and Korea.
- Independent reservoir data from Cape Station.
- Multi-year metered output from any wave array.
- A complete mass budget for an orbital compute satellite.
