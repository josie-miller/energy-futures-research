# BLUE PLANET: why the ocean powers the next two centuries

*A long-range vision for the scrolling site. This is not a forecast. It is a scenario designed to stay inside published physical limits. Every number is either sourced or calculated (see "Where the numbers come from"). The 2050 column is the Stage 3 model result; 2100 and 2200 are designed scenarios checked against physics guardrails.*

---

## The one-line pitch

**Land is where we live. The sun is where energy comes from. The ocean is where we can collect it, store it, cool it, fuel it, and ship it, on 71% of the planet's surface.**

Solar wins the next 25 years on land. Over the next 175 years the binding limits change from cost to **space, fuel, heat and geography**, and on every one of them the ocean has the largest margin.

## Chapter 1: 2050, the coastal frontier (modelled)
This is the Stage 3 result:
- Solar leads with about half of world electricity.
- The ocean reaches about 10% of world supply.
- At strong-tide coasts the ocean already supplies 84%, and around the North Sea about a third.
- Offshore wind must ramp from 9.3 to about 74–100 GW per year.

**This is where the ocean industry gets built**: ports, installation vessels, floating foundations, undersea high-voltage DC (HVDC) cables.

## Chapter 2: 2100, the Blue Grid (designed scenario)
Ocean collectors supply **46%** of 215,000 TWh/yr. That is roughly 7× all of today's electricity.
- **Far-offshore wind** (~8 TW). Floating turbines follow the storm tracks, where Possner & Caldeira (2017) find that <cite index="347-6">downward transport of kinetic energy may sustain extraction rates of 6 W m^−2 and above over large areas in the annual mean</cite>, against the roughly 1.5 W/m² limit usually quoted for large wind farms. Footprint: 0.37–1.5% of the ocean.
- **Offshore floating solar between the turbines.** This already exists: <cite index="387-5">using just 3–5% of the space between turbines for solar panels can boost energy output by over 20%</cite>. China's 1 GW offshore array covers 1,223 ha. At that density (12.3 W/m² average), 2100's offshore solar uses just 0.05% of the ocean.
- **OTEC (ocean thermal energy conversion) belts in the tropics** (~0.9 TW, 13% of the 7 TW no-harm ceiling). These are 24/7 plants that ship green ammonia. Seaborne trade is already the backbone: the WMO notes <cite index="392-5">around 80% of international trade in goods by volume is carried by sea</cite>.
- **Tidal stream on every strong strait.** It is the grid's clock: predictable centuries ahead and uncorrelated with wind (r = 0.01 in our Orkney data).
- **A meshed undersea supergrid.** It links storm tracks, trade-wind belts and tidal straits on different weather systems, so one region's calm is another's gale.
- **Compute moves to sea.** Hainan's commercial undersea data centre is reported as <cite index="362-4">40-60 percent more power efficient than traditional facilities</cite>. Shanghai's wind-powered version reports <cite index="365-5">at least 30% less electricity than traditional data centers</cite>.

## Chapter 3: 2200, the Ocean Civilization (designed scenario)
Ocean collectors supply **64%** of 582,000 TWh/yr (66 TW). Counting fission and fusion fuelled from seawater, **73% of civilization runs on the sea.**
- **~21 TW of open-ocean wind**: 0.9–3.8% of the ocean surface.
- **Offshore solar archipelagos**: 0.39% of the ocean.
- **OTEC**: 4.6 TW, still under the 7 TW no-harm ceiling.
- **Fusion, if it arrives, is ocean energy too.** Its fuel is deuterium filtered from seawater, and the ocean holds about **1.1 billion years** of supply even at 178 TW (panel b below).
- **Fission's fuel also comes from the sea.** A 2025 *Nature Communications* paper notes <cite index="355-11">the ocean contains approximately 4.5 billion tons of dissolved uranium, which is about 1000 times the known terrestrial uranium reserves</cite>.

## Seven reasons the ocean is the best long-run energy system

1. **Space. It is 71% of the planet, and nobody lives on it.** All of 2025's electricity could come from floating solar on **0.074% of the ocean** (≈266,000 km²). Each 1% of the ocean under floating solar yields ~388,000 TWh/yr, about 5× the 2050 demand.
2. **Better wind.** Ocean storm tracks pull energy down from the whole atmosphere. Possner & Caldeira estimate up to ~6 W/m² in the North Atlantic, about 4× the usual onshore limit.
3. **Rhythm.** Tides are set by orbital mechanics and can be predicted centuries ahead. OTEC runs at about a 92% capacity factor in our Oʻahu data. Wave and wind are only partly correlated (r = 0.41). The ocean brings its own baseload and its own diversity.
4. **The fuel tank.** It holds uranium for about 26,000 years in fast reactors, and deuterium for about a billion years, at 178 TW. Whichever firm technology wins, its fuel is in the sea.
5. **The heat sink.** Cooling 1 GW of servers in orbit needs **~1.2 km² of radiator** (300 K, two-sided, emissivity 0.9). In the sea it needs **245 m³/s of seawater warmed by 1 °C**, the flow of a small river, at zero launch cost. This is the physics answer to orbital data centres.
6. **Next to the people.** The UN notes <cite index="391-4">about 40% of the world's population lives within 100 kilometers of the coast</cite>. The ocean supplies coastal megacities without long transmission corridors over land.
7. **Thermodynamics. Ocean flows add no new heat.** Wind, tides, waves and ocean heat would have dissipated as heat anyway. Fission and fusion release new heat. Today waste heat is only <cite index="379-5">+0.028 W m^−2</cite> globally (Flanner 2009). An all-fusion 2200 would add **0.33–0.87 W/m²**, which is 11–30% of today's ~2.9 W/m² greenhouse forcing. The Blue Planet mix adds only **0.03 W/m²**. At civilization scale, **the ocean is the only energy system that can grow without becoming a new climate forcing.**

## Physical guardrails (what keeps this from being science fiction)

| Check | 2100 | 2200 | Limit used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ocean area under wind farms | 0.37–1.5% | 0.95–3.8% | 1.5–6 W/m² (Possner & Caldeira 2017) |
| Ocean area under floating solar | 0.05% | 0.39% | Local coverage below 20% (see below) |
| OTEC versus 7 TW no-harm ceiling | 13% | 65% | Rajagopalan & Nihous 2013 |
| Tidal, wave, salinity | at resource bounds | at resource bounds | 110 / 850 / 625 TWh/yr |
| Heat added by fission + fusion | 0.007 W/m² | 0.029 W/m² | Compared with today's 0.028 W/m² |

On the floating-solar coverage limit, one North Sea case study found that <cite index="382-7,382-8">if the surface area covered was smaller than 20%, there wasn't much of an impact – less than 10% change to the primary productivity. However, above 40% of surface coverage, researchers saw serious ecosystem distress</cite>.

## What has to become true (milestones for the website timeline)
1. **2030s:** floating wind reaches about $74/MWh (DOE target). Tidal stream passes about 3 GW built, which takes it below $100/MWh on a 17% learning rate.
2. **2040s:** offshore wind is built at more than 75 GW/yr. The first meshed HVDC offshore hubs and energy islands are running.
3. **2050s:** offshore floating solar falls from today's estimated <cite index="384-6">levelised cost of energy (LCOE) of 354 €/MWh</cite> to below about €60/MWh. Commercial OTEC plants above 100 MW ship ammonia.
4. **2070s:** seawater uranium becomes cost-competitive. This is the 2100 chapter's assumption, not an observed result: today's adsorbents are lab-stage (e.g., <cite index="357-2">14.62 mg-U/g-Ads in natural seawater after 56 days</cite>).
5. **2100+:** far-offshore autonomous wind and solar fleets maintained by robots. If fusion works, plants sit offshore, fed and cooled by the sea.

## What could break the vision (say this on the site; it builds trust)
- **Cost and survival at sea.** Corrosion, storms and maintenance make every ocean device costlier than its land twin today. Offshore floating solar is currently ~5–8× land solar.
- **Ecology.** Large-scale wind extraction changes regional climate. OTEC above about 7 TW cools the surface ocean. Dense floating solar shades plankton. All three need limits like the guardrails above.
- **Governance.** Beyond the 200-nautical-mile exclusive economic zones (EEZs) the high seas have no energy-permitting regime. 2200's ocean grid needs a new UNCLOS-style treaty.
- **Land solar remains a strong competitor.** Deserts alone could host enormous solar. The ocean's case rests on space, cooling, fuel, heat and coastal proximity, not on solar being scarce.

## Off-Earth hook (preview of Stage 5)
The answer changes off Earth, because **Earth's ocean is unique**. In orbit, solar is the natural choice, but heat rejection costs ~1.2 km² of radiator per GW. On the Moon and Mars there are no liquid oceans, so fission plus solar is the answer. The far-future twist: icy moons like Europa and Enceladus have subsurface oceans heated by tides. **Wherever there is an ocean, there is energy.**
