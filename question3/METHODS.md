# Q3 methods and reproduction

Research date: 24 October 2026. Main answer: [ANSWER.md](ANSWER.md).

## Three different kinds of result

1. **Proposed global portfolio.** We allocate 60,000 and 80,000 TWh/year across technologies, then calculate implied capacity and construction rates. These allocations are recommendations to test, not outputs of the coastal optimizer.
2. **Hourly coastal experiment.** We minimize modeled supply costs for an isolated 1 GW flat load with supplied weather/current profiles. Default checked runs require zero unmet demand in every modeled hour. This does not establish real-world grid reliability.
3. **Input and claim audit.** We reproduce the supplied preprocessing, inspect original optimization results and distinguish observed, modeled and proposed quantities.

## Global arithmetic

`TWh/year = electrical GW × 8.76 × average-output fraction`

`GW required = TWh/year ÷ (8.76 × average-output fraction)`

`annual growth = (2050 generation / 2024 generation)^(1/26) − 1`

`average net offshore additions = (2050 capacity − 2025 capacity) / 25`

Use 31,229 TWh for the consistent 2024 generation baseline. The rounded planning cases yield 2.54% and 3.68% annual growth; exact WEO scenario rates remain in Q1. Offshore baseline is 92.5 GW at end-2025. All other capacity factors and portfolio allocations are explicit in `proposal_inputs.json`.

Generation is not final consumption. We do not claim that 60,000 TWh generated by this new portfolio serves the identical end-use demand as 60,000 TWh from a different portfolio with different losses. A regional full-system calculation must reconcile that difference. Storage is excluded from generation shares.

## Hourly model

The model minimizes annualized generator capacity costs, storage energy and power costs, and nuclear variable cost. It has perfect foresight of the complete supplied period. Storage states are cyclic across the modeled period, which prevents a free initial energy stock without a matching final stock. For multiple years, the cycle closes across the whole horizon, not separately each year.

Generation and power capacities use **GW**; storage energy uses **GWh**; hourly output uses **GWh**; annual fixed costs use **USD/kW-year** or **USD/kWh-year**; variable costs and total supply cost use **USD/MWh**. With capacities in GW, fixed-cost coefficients numerically yield USD million/year. Annual costs divide by the same modeled load and time horizon to report USD/MWh. Leap hours are included in actual modeled energy; annualization uses the original model's 8,760-hour-year convention.

Battery round-trip efficiency is 0.94² = 88.36%. Long-duration storage round-trip efficiency is 0.63² = 39.69%. The stored-energy capacity is an internal state; electricity delivered from a full store is lower after discharge losses. A capacity divided by discharge rating is therefore not automatically usable customer duration.

Unless specified, costs and technologies come from the supplied 2035 input set. The checked named-price cases calibrate annual generator fixed costs to the **common 2022–24 mean profile**, so the stated price has an explicit basis. Weather and tidal-output stress cases retain the same annual cost per installed kW as the central Scottish reference. This avoids silently making equipment cheaper in a lower-output stress case.

The $74/MWh floating price, tidal price sweeps, $80/MWh wave case and $100/MWh idealized firm option are assumptions. They are not bids, current observed prices or guaranteed 2035 outcomes. Other supplied fixed costs remain unchanged. Costs are interpreted in the supplied 2025-dollar framework, whose individual source conversions have not all been independently reconstructed.

Nuclear's default maximum output is 90% of installed capacity every hour. The cheap-firm sensitivity uses that same simplified availability and cost structure. Neither represents stochastic outages, construction risk or a real geothermal/nuclear contract. The model excludes transmission, imports, reservoir hydro, reserves, ramp constraints, minimum generation, maintenance, wake/resource limits and demand flexibility. These are material boundaries.

The fast formulation allows renewable output to be discarded implicitly. It attributes used renewable output proportionally to each technology's available output in each hour. It solves the same cost problem as explicit dispatch without a generation-share rule; attribution among zero-variable-cost technologies is not unique. The marine-share case uses explicit generation variables and constrains its share of dispatched primary generation, including energy sent into storage. Neither attribution is a traced final-customer energy mix.

Checked Hawaii runs cap the supplied OTEC profile at 100% of a hard AC capacity rating. Original inputs are preserved. The 2010 case is labeled synthetic because it uses 2023 tidal timing. See the [audit](MODEL_AUDIT.md) for missing-data treatment and other issues.

## Run

From the repository root, using an isolated Python environment:

```sh
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python question3/test_model.py
python question3/run_experiments.py --workers 2
python question3/analyze_proposal.py
python question3/summarize_experiments.py
```

To run selected cases, use `--only` followed by IDs from `experiment_jobs.json`. Runs write separate JSON files in `experiments/`, including the exact costs, input hash, output, status and physical residual checks. Failed or timed-out cases are not valid optimal solutions. The current HiGHS simplex solver runs one thread per process; optimal status and physical residual checks are required.

The original preprocessor uses relative folders `ocean_data/` and `ocean_model/`. For its reproduction, place copies or links to the original raw downloads in the former and a writable output directory in the latter. Execute a copy of `inputs/Unconfirmed 462875.crdownload` as Python from that working directory. Do not overwrite archived inputs. We performed this reproduction separately and compared all five rebuilt profiles to the originals.

## Checks performed

- Portfolio sums equal the two generation targets, with ocean exactly 15% and direct marine generation 0.5%.
- Capacity conversions, annual growth rates and construction rates are explicit calculations rather than fitted forecasts.
- Original upload hashes and bytes match the supplied files.
- Profiles have no duplicate or missing modeled hours; raw-data gaps are disclosed rather than hidden by the processed profiles.
- Small hand-calculable tests check generator costs, storage losses, no-supply infeasibility and the dispatched marine-share rule.
- Successful research runs check storage dynamics, capacity limits, hourly balance and annual energy conservation. Unserved energy is required to be zero in the default cases.
- Figures are rendered and visually inspected. Supporting values remain in JSON.

These checks establish arithmetic and computational consistency. They do not establish equipment reliability, source-model accuracy, the best real site, construction feasibility or global optimality. Final investment decisions require validated regional data and full project economics.
